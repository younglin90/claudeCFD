Abstract
In the numerical simulation of fluid dynamic problems there are situations in which acoustic waves are very fast compared
to the average velocity of the fluid and conversely situations in which the fluid moves at high speed and shock waves may
be present. Ideally, a numerical method should be able to treat these different regimes without strong limitations in terms of
time step and without excessive related computational cost. Unfortunately, standard explicit in time schemes often adopted for
hyperbolic problems are not suitable for these problems, hence remedies have to be studied. To this aim, the results presented in
this article concern the development of a second order in time and space numerical method for the compressible Navier–Stokes
equation which works for both high and low Mach numbers. In particular, when the Mach number goes to zero, one recovers a
numerical method for the limit Navier–Stokes system which under some additional hypothesis degenerates to the incompressible
Navier–Stokes equations, while in the case of high Mach numbers the method exhibits a shock capturing structure. The idea
is based on partitioning the equations into a fast and a slow scale and by taking implicit the fast scale dynamic together with
the viscous terms. The resulting numerical scheme is stable for time steps which are independent both from the speed of the
pressure waves and from the diffusive terms characterizing the viscous forces and the heat flux. The only time step limitation
is induced by the average speed of the flow. The work here presented extends the seminal ideas developed in Dimarco et al.
(2017, 2018) for isentropic Euler equations and in Boscheri et al. (2020) for the full set of compressible Euler equations to
the multidimensional Navier–Stokes system and permits efficient three dimensional simulations of all Mach problems. The
discretization is constructed on Cartesian meshes and the method is second order accurate in space and time. Numerical results
show the accuracy, the robustness and the effectiveness of the new proposed approach.

properties of the system and typically lead to equation of conservation of mass, momentum and total energy under
different forms. The most known example is represented by the so-called compressible Navier–Stokes equations [3].
Starting from this system it is possible, under different hypothesis, to derive different simplified set of equations
such as the compressible Euler equations in the inviscid case or the incompressible Navier–Stokes equations in the
limit in which the Mach number goes to zero and the fluid is isentropic. In this work, we are particularly interested
in the construction of a numerical method able to describe the passage from the compressible to the zero Mach
limit. In the general case of Navier–Stokes equations this does not necessarily yield an incompressible regime as
recalled after, this latter being recovered under the additional assumption of isentropic or inviscid flows. Besides this
fact, the Mach number measures the ratio between the fluid velocity and the sound speed and may vary of several
order of magnitude depending on the problem under examination. This variation is also possible at different times
or space position during the same simulation. In particular, the incompressible limit, in the isentropic or inviscid
case, can be obtained when the Mach number takes values tending to zero [3].
In the last years, for the case of strongly compressible flows a very large number of different families of explicit
finite difference and Godunov-type finite volume schemes have been developed, see for example [4,5] and the
references therein. Instead, the type of approach that has been pursued in the field of the incompressible flows has
been quite different due to the type of governing equations that describe this opposite situation. Typically, due to
the elliptic nature of the problem, semi-implicit methods are preferred in this latter case [6–9].
From a theoretical point of view, the passage from incompressible to compressible scaling and vice versa has
been the subject of many studies [10–15]. Also, from the point of view of the applications, many efforts have been
done in the recent past to construct numerical methods which are able to deal both with the incompressible and the
compressible regimes, see the seminal work of [7] and other more recent research contributions [16–21]. In this
context, many studies have been sacred to understand the analytical properties of the numerical methods [19,22–25],
but most of these results are limited both from the theoretical as well as from the numerical side to isentropic [26–28]
or inviscid flows [29–33]. Some more recent results have been obtained in [34–37] which deal with the compressible
Navier–Stokes or free surface equations and/or make use of unstructured and staggered grids. Semi-implicit
discretizations for high order discontinuous Galerkin (DG) and hybrid finite volume/finite element schemes can be
found in [38–42]. Here, we intend to go further and, to this aim, we follow the path traced in the seminal work [23]
and continued in [43,44]. In these series of articles, the authors realized efficient and high order numerical methods
which were able to deal with all Mach regimes with the additional property that the time step restrictions were
independent on the physical regime. In particular, the contribution of the present article consists in the extension
to the case of the full system of compressible Navier–Stokes equations and in the three dimensional setting of the
results obtained in [43]. Let us observe that while the compressible equations describe the conservation of density,
momentum and energy, the corresponding zero Mach model deals with a constraint on the divergence of the velocity
field which has to match the divergence of the temperature, with an elliptic equation for the pressure and with a
transport equation for the density moving at the average speed of the flow. These two models can be connected
analytically by a rescaling procedure, while a choice of the time and space integrators has to be done to have at
disposal a suitable numerical method which overcomes the stiffness of the resulting equations. A first idea would
consist in taking all terms implicitly in the Navier–Stokes system. Unfortunately, it is well known that implicit
schemes are not simple to be applied due to the non linearity of the resulting discrete equations. Furthermore, the
necessity to employ high order in space schemes complicates the realization of such methods. This is precisely the
motivation which stays behind our choice of a semi-implicit approach which consists in treating implicitly only
one part of the system to be solved while keeping the remaining part explicit. To that aim, we consider a particular
class of Implicit–Explicit time integrators by taking in implicit all components responsible for appearance of the fast
pressure waves. At the same time, we also treat the viscous terms implicitly in order to avoid a diffusive and very
restrictive CFL condition caused by this dynamic while we keep the remaining terms explicit. The main difficulties
which arise with such approach are due to the treatment of the viscous terms in a semi-implicit setting, as detailed
in the rest of the paper. Besides this complication, our approach permits to overcome the stiffness of the equations
and to describe all Mach flows with the same numerical method and time step. Moreover, the algorithm proposed
in this work diminishes the complexity of the non linear systems to be inverted in the discrete setting in order
to advance the solution in time. Our method belongs to the class of schemes known as Implicit–Explicit (IMEX)

choose the time step independently from both the Mach number and the viscous terms turns in very important
advantages in practical applications from the computational viewpoint.

3.2. On the asymptotic preserving property of the first order in time IMEX method
We start by recalling the following definition in agreement with [27,44,47].
Definition 1. A consistent time discretization method for (22)–(23) supplemented by the equation of state (14) of
time step ∆t is said
- Strong asymptotic preserving (SAP) if, independently of the initial data, it is asymptotically stable and
consistent. That is, it is stable for time steps ∆t which can be chosen independently of ε, and it becomes
a consistent time discretization method for the zero Mach limit system (15)–(17) in the limit ε →0.
- Asymptotically accurate if the order of accuracy in time in the stiff limit ε →0 is maintained.

with the limit model. We then have the following additional definition.

5. Conclusions and perspectives
In this article an efficient semi-implicit finite volume solver for compressible flows has been presented. The
method is based on the flux splitting of the hyperbolic part of the governing equations which allows the stiff and
the non stiff part of the system to be properly decoupled and differently discretized. Specifically, all terms involving
slow scale phenomena are treated implicitly, whereas an explicit approach is adopted for handling the fast scale
waves. All advection terms are numerically solved by an explicit finite volume solver based on Rusanov numerical
fluxes, hence ensuring shock capturing properties in high Mach number flows. Central difference operators are used
for the approximation of implicit pressure fluxes in momentum and energy equations, thus yielding a relaxed CFL

Navier–Stokes equations are also treated implicitly, so that the numerical stability of the scheme only depends on
the main fluid speed, improving the efficiency of the algorithm even in viscous dominated problems. On that aim,
a novel iterative algorithm has been designed, that is based on two nested Picard iterative procedures, in which the
first external one controls the convergence of the viscous terms, while the second internal one is responsible about
the resolution of a pressure wave equation. This equation results from the formal substitution of the momentum
into the energy equation, thus leading to a semi-implicit method. Moreover, second order of accuracy in space is
achieved relying on a classical piecewise linear reconstruction of the conserved variables supplemented by minmod
limiter. IMEX Runge–Kutta time stepping guarantees that the novel methods are fully second order accurate even
in time. Furthermore, asymptotic preserving properties are likely to be respected in the low Mach limit at the
discrete level. A proof of the asymptotic preserving (AP) property of the scheme for the semi-discrete first order
discretization has been derived. A suite of test cases demonstrates the accuracy, the robustness and the effectiveness
of the novel pressure based implicit–explicit (PV-IMEX) schemes, solving low and high Mach flows, as well as
smooth and discontinuous profiles involving shock waves. Space and time convergence studies demonstrate that the
formal order of accuracy is correctly achieved and numerical evidences concerning the advantages of the implicit
discretization of viscous terms are provided.
The novel PV-IMEX scheme has been implemented on three-dimensional Cartesian grids and makes use of MPI
parallelization. The reconstruction and pressure stencils involve a total number of five elements per dimension, thus
making the scheme relatively compact and suitable for parallel simulations.
Further work will concern the investigation of the theoretical Asymptotic Preserving property of the novel
algorithm as well as the extension to higher order of accuracy in space and time, which allows the size of the
computational mesh to be reduced by increasing the accuracy of the numerical solution. As such, this would lead
towards an optimization of the all Mach solvers, reducing the computational efforts.
